# Figure legends

**Figure 1. Pretreatment myocardial mechanical-transition phenotype and construction of Mechanical Transition Instability.** (A) Median pretreatment Tissue Doppler velocity trajectories in women who subsequently developed cancer therapy-related cardiac dysfunction (CTRCD) and those who did not, with interquartile bands. S, E′, and A′ indicate systolic, early-diastolic, and late-diastolic myocardial velocity phases. (B) Mechanical Transition Instability (MTI) is the equal-weight mean of 4 training-standardized measures that characterize the transition from systolic motion into early relaxation: transition slope deficit, transition curvature deficit, early-relaxation latency, and E′ attenuation. Higher MTI indicates a slower, blunted, delayed, and lower-amplitude transition into early relaxation. (C) Patient-level waveform atlas ordered by MTI illustrates continuous variation in the pretreatment mechanical phenotype rather than a discrete threshold. (D) Phase-resolved difference between event and nonevent waveforms with bootstrap uncertainty. Whole-waveform functional permutation testing yielded P=0.047 before LVEF adjustment and P=0.056 after residualizing for baseline LVEF. The strongest contiguous difference occurred near normalized phase 0.499 to 0.600 but did not survive cluster-wise family-error correction (FWER P=0.094), supporting interpretation as a distributed transition phenotype rather than a statistically isolated phase interval. TDI indicates Tissue Doppler imaging.

**Figure 2. Clinical association, discrimination, and surveillance risk enrichment of Mechanical Transition Instability.** (A) Receiver-operating-characteristic curves from predictions aggregated across 100 repeated stratified 5-fold cross-validation partitions. MTI standardization and model fitting were restricted to training folds. (B) CTRCD-free survival across increasing MTI tertiles; event counts were 5/90, 6/90, and 16/90 in the low, middle, and high tertiles, respectively. Tertiles are shown for visualization and do not represent validated clinical cutoffs. (C) Hazard ratios per 1-SD higher MTI from Cox proportional hazards models, including adjustment for baseline LVEF, age, treatment exposures, and the preserved-LVEF sensitivity cohort. (D) Exploratory surveillance-efficiency curve generated exclusively from cross-fitted MTI + LVEF risk rankings. Selecting the highest-risk 20% of women identified 13 of 27 subsequent CTRCD events (48.1%) with a positive predictive value of 24.1%, compared with 10.0% event prevalence in the full TDI cohort. This panel evaluates risk concentration and does not define a clinical intervention threshold. CI indicates confidence interval; CTRCD, cancer therapy-related cardiac dysfunction; LVEF, left ventricular ejection fraction; MTI, Mechanical Transition Instability.
